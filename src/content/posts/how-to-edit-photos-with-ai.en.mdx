You do not need desktop software to clean up a product shot, soften a portrait, or erase a passerby. An **AI photo editor** lets you describe the change in everyday language and generate a new version in the browser.

## The basic workflow

1. **Choose a clear photo** — JPG, PNG, or WebP works well. Good lighting and a sharp subject usually produce more reliable edits.
2. **Describe what should change** — Be specific: “replace the background with a soft studio gray” beats “make it better.”
3. **Generate, then inspect** — Check faces, edges, text, and small products before you download or share.
4. **Refine if needed** — Adjust the prompt and try again when a detail looks off.

On GetAIPhotoEditor you can prepare the image and prompt before signing in. Generating a standard 1K edit uses credits after you sign in.

## Common edits that work well

- **Background changes** — Studio, outdoor scenes, or cleaner e-commerce backdrops. Watch hair edges and reflections.
- **Object removal** — Ask to remove a specific item and reconstruct the area behind it. Crowded scenes may need a second pass.
- **Portrait retouch** — Soft skin cleanup or lighting tweaks while keeping the person recognizable.
- **Enhancement** — Brighter light, clearer colors, or a more polished look. Generative enhancement is not lossless recovery of lost detail.

## Tips for better prompts

- Name the **subject** you want to keep unchanged.
- Describe the **new look** in concrete terms (lighting, place, mood).
- Mention what must **stay the same** (face, logo, product shape).
- Avoid vague words like “fix” or “improve” without a target.

## When to use traditional tools instead

AI editing is strong for fast creative changes. Pixel-perfect masks, batch catalogs with identical crops, and print-critical color work still favor desktop editors. Many people use both: AI for a first pass, then a traditional tool for final control.
